      Renting guide

      Common questions about leases and renting. Tap a question to see the answer.

      When should I start looking for next year's apartment?

      In a college town, many places for the next school year are leased months ahead, often in the fall or winter before. Start early so you have choices, but don't sign until you've compared a few places.

      What should I read in a lease before signing?

      Check the lease dates, the monthly rent and when it's due, late fees, which utilities you pay, who handles repairs, the rules on subletting, and what happens if you need to leave early. If something you were promised out loud isn't written in the lease, ask for it in writing.

      What does "jointly and severally liable" mean?

      It's common in roommate leases. Each roommate can be held responsible for the whole rent, not just their share, so if one roommate stops paying, the others may have to cover it. Choose roommates you trust.

      How does a security deposit work?

      You pay it up front, and the landlord can keep part of it for unpaid rent or damage beyond normal wear. Take dated photos of every room when you move in and when you move out, so you can show what was already there.

      What costs come on top of rent?

      Look for utilities (electric, gas, water, internet), parking, application and admin fees, and renter's insurance. The score on the My apartments tab spreads some of these into a monthly total so places are easier to compare.

      What should I ask on a tour?

      Ask how repairs get requested and how fast they're done, what the average utility bill is, whether there's laundry in the unit, how loud it gets, and whether you can see the actual unit, not a model.

      Where can I get help with a lease question?

      Many universities offer free legal help for students, including lease reviews. Check what your school provides before you sign anything you're unsure about.
